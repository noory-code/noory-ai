## User intent and current constraints

The user wants Avatar to get to know a person through their conversations, somewhat like the
process of getting to know someone in counseling. This is a product analogy, not evidence of
clinical equivalence. Decisions, vocabulary, kindness of expression, concise or long explanations,
and reactions are clues that should connect into an understanding of the person. A checklist of
counts alone does not satisfy the purpose. The user wants a playful service with reports and an
avatar, and wants useful ideas retained. Existing conversations are the source; a new questionnaire
must not become the main product. Repeated questions must not interrupt the person's work.

The latest business direction is a global service supported by ordinary banner advertising,
such as Google AdSense. Earlier paid-report and subscription proposals are superseded. Ad-network
engineering is not today's main design task. Preserve the prior constraint: the operator should
not collect private work conversations or personal profiles. No permission has been given to
send derived profiles to advertisers. Global launch and advertising integration are not implemented.

The local prototype can import explicit Avatar choice records and request optional Claude analysis.
It does not yet analyze general conversation, establish a personality assessment, or use a fully
local model. These are supervisor-supplied implementation facts, not independently verified here.

## Public research notes supplied to the debate

These are brief summaries of inspected primary research or official documentation. They support
specific questions, not a claim that Avatar works. The reviewer has no browsing tools; do not claim
to have independently verified the sources. Treat the summaries as supplied evidence.

- S1: Peters, Cerf, and Matz (2024 preprint), GPT-4 study with 566 US participants and 15-turn interactions. Mean correlations with self-reported Big Five scores were about .443 in assessment-oriented conversation and .117 in the helpful-assistant condition. Correlation is not an individual accuracy percentage. https://arxiv.org/html/2405.13052v1 . We inspected the abstract and methods. The study used assessment-directed prompts; do not adopt concealed assessment instructions as product policy.
- S2: Zhu, Jin, and Coifman (2025 preprint) reports 555 semi-structured interviews. Its abstract reports weak correlations (maximum r=.27) despite high repeatability across model runs. Different samples, models, and measures prevent a direct pooled conclusion with S1. https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.14355 . Abstract inspected; this is not a full methods audit.
- S3: Forer (1949), original article reproduced at https://criticalthinkingtext.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/04/forer_the-fallacy-of-personal-validation_1949.pdf . The inspected text explains why acceptance of broadly applicable descriptions does not validate the inference method. Do not treat a user's feeling of recognition as the sole quality measure.
- S4: Brown (2015), author-affiliated research abstract: Korean honorifics can convey context-specific meanings, including sarcasm; fixed deference labels are insufficient. https://research.monash.edu/en/publications/expressive-social-and-gendered-meanings-of-korean-honorifics/ . This does not supply a tested multilingual scoring algorithm.
- S5: Anthropic (2023) reports sycophancy across several assistants and tasks, including preference for convincing agreement over correctness in some judgments. https://www.anthropic.com/research/towards-understanding-sycophancy-in-language-models . It does not measure Avatar or the models installed today.
- S6: Google states non-personalized ads still use cookies or mobile identifiers for frequency capping and aggregate reporting. https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/9007336?hl=en . Ordinary ads therefore do not justify an absolute claim of zero data collection by every party. Private work content and ad-related technical metadata need separate descriptions.

## Round 1 — Codex (desktop) — 2026-10-07

### Points

- P1: Build a revisable, context-dependent portrait: how a person frames problems, explains needs, handles disagreement, and states priorities. These patterns can connect into candidate explanations, but the product should distinguish observed behavior from inferred motives and general personality claims. Hypothesis: this delivers the user's desired depth better than a checklist or fixed type. S1/S2 justify testing, not presuming accuracy.
- P2: The unit of analysis should be an interaction episode, retaining who said what, the task, the AI's preceding behavior, and stated constraints. Otherwise an AI's repeated mistakes may look like the user's repetitive style. This is a design inference to test with cases A and B below; source references are not speaker authentication.
- P3: A profile claim should retain supporting episodes, exceptions, alternative explanations, scope, and update history. Confidence should describe evidence coverage and known limits; do not display invented probabilities. A new contradiction might narrow the scope rather than erase everything or label the person inconsistent. Proposed architecture, not validated psychology.
- P4: The analysis can be passive within a scope the user has selected, with occasional optional reflection after work. It must not covertly shape work conversations to extract personality information. S1's better result under assessment-directed dialogue does not justify that product choice. No collection mechanism is approved or implemented by this discussion.
- P5: For a global product, preserve the original language and context. Test each supported language's interpretations, including code-switching, honorifics, short-message fragmentation, and translation. Do not infer nationality or rank kindness across languages. Global ambition does not establish equal analysis quality in every language; S4 motivates a Korean challenge case.
- P6: A useful portrait must outperform a well-presented evidence list and a generic flattering description in some measurable way. Candidate checks: correct source attribution, response to counterevidence, source-specific recognition, and later use of a supported distinction. Satisfaction, agreement, and repeated model outputs are separate measures, not correctness. S2/S3/S5 motivate this separation.
- P7: Keep the experience warm and playful. Candidate products include situation-based portrait cards, a view of changed interpretations, and a way to try a chosen communication style. Depth must remain visible without making the default screen a technical audit log. These are untested product ideas. Challenge whether caution has made the proposal too bland to use.
- P8: The business is a global, ad-supported service. A possible first layout keeps network ads on public introduction/example pages and a local private profile view without third-party scripts. This is an implementation proposal, not a new business model. The tradeoff is fewer ad impressions. Do not invent revenue rates or provider approval. Limit ad discussion to concrete constraints and feasibility.

### Fictional cases for adversarial reasoning

- A: An assistant ignores two explicitly stated requirements. The user restates them in a long message. Another long message from the same user voluntarily explores several possible product directions. Do not equate both lengths with the same reason for elaboration.
- B: The user says "short answer please." The assistant gives a long answer. The user then provides a long explanation of why it failed. Which utterance is a response preference, which is corrective behavior, and which is evidence of a general habit?
- C: Korean: "참 잘하셨네요. 하지 말라고 한 걸 또 하셨군요." It uses polite endings and literal praise while criticizing. Compare a short friendly "고마워!". Explain what remains ambiguous without diagnosing character.
- D: "I need more detail" appears after a risky migration plan, while "just tell me the result" appears after a routine build. A fixed concise/verbose personality label may conceal a conditional pattern.
- E: A transcript contains a pasted fictional villain's monologue and an AI-drafted email accepted for a one-off purpose. Attribute the words before inferring the user's usual tone.
- F: A user tries a deliberately blunt persona for seven days, then stops. The profile must distinguish the intervention from a spontaneous long-term shift.
- G: A saved portrait says the user wants detailed explanations. New explicit instruction says "For today's deployment, one sentence is enough." A persistent profile must not override the current request or count its own influence as independent confirmation.
- H: Only summaries remain after source deletion. Some summaries use approving adjectives, and an updated model changes its wording. Do not silently strengthen the profile or claim evidence is still auditable.
