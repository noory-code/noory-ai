## User intent and current constraints

The user wants Avatar to get to know a person through their conversations, somewhat like the
process of getting to know someone in counseling. This is a product analogy, not evidence of
clinical equivalence. Decisions, vocabulary, kindness of expression, concise or long explanations,
and reactions are clues that should connect into an understanding of the person. A checklist of
counts alone does not satisfy the purpose. The user wants a playful service with reports and an
avatar, and wants useful ideas retained. Existing conversations are the source; a new questionnaire
must not become the main product. Repeated questions must not interrupt the person's work.

The latest business direction is a global service supported by ordinary banner advertising,
such as Google AdSense. Earlier paid-report and subscription proposals are superseded. Ad-network
engineering is not today's main design task. Preserve the prior constraint: the operator should
not collect private work conversations or personal profiles. No permission has been given to
send derived profiles to advertisers. Global launch and advertising integration are not implemented.

The local prototype can import explicit Avatar choice records and request optional Claude analysis.
It does not yet analyze general conversation, establish a personality assessment, or use a fully

## Public research notes supplied to the debate

These are brief summaries of inspected primary research or official documentation. They support
specific questions, not a claim that Avatar works. The reviewer has no browsing tools; do not claim

- S1: Peters, Cerf, and Matz (2024 preprint), GPT-4 study with 566 US participants and 15-turn interactions. Mean correlations with self-reported Big Five scores were about .443 in assessment-oriented conversation and .117 in the helpful-assistant condition. Correlation is not an individual accuracy percentage. https://arxiv.org/html/2405.13052v1 . We inspected the abstract and methods. The study used assessment-directed prompts; do not adopt concealed assessment instructions as product policy.
- S2: Zhu, Jin, and Coifman (2025 preprint) reports 555 semi-structured interviews. Its abstract reports weak correlations (maximum r=.27) despite high repeatability across model runs. Different samples, models, and measures prevent a direct pooled conclusion with S1. https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.14355 . Abstract inspected; this is not a full methods audit.
- S3: Forer (1949), original article reproduced at https://criticalthinkingtext.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/04/forer_the-fallacy-of-personal-validation_1949.pdf . The inspected text explains why acceptance of broadly applicable descriptions does not validate the inference method. Do not treat a user's feeling of recognition as the sole quality measure.
- S4: Brown (2015), author-affiliated research abstract: Korean honorifics can convey context-specific meanings, including sarcasm; fixed deference labels are insufficient. https://research.monash.edu/en/publications/expressive-social-and-gendered-meanings-of-korean-honorifics/ . This does not supply a tested multilingual scoring algorithm.
- S5: Anthropic (2023) reports sycophancy across several assistants and tasks, including preference for convincing agreement over correctness in some judgments. https://www.anthropic.com/research/towards-understanding-sycophancy-in-language-models . It does not measure Avatar or the models installed today.
- S6: Google states non-personalized ads still use cookies or mobile identifiers for frequency capping and aggregate reporting. https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/9007336?hl=en . ### Points

- P1: Build a revisable, context-dependent portrait: how a person frames problems, explains needs, handles disagreement, and states priorities. - P3: A profile claim should retain supporting episodes, exceptions, alternative explanations, scope, and update history. ### Strongest case FOR a rich profile

A list of evidence can't hold a *condition*. Case D, "detail when the stakes are high, brevity when the task is routine," exists only as an interpretation that links episodes together. No count of words per message will show it. Without some synthesis, the user gets data rather than understanding, and the product is pointless (inference).

### Strongest case AGAINST

In ordinary use the signal is weak (.117, literature). A fluent portrait may therefore be mostly the model's priors, and Forer acceptance plus sycophancy would make users approve of it anyway (S3, S5, literature). A product could get high satisfaction while being close to a horoscope, and satisfaction metrics would never show it (inference).

**Where I land:** both are right. So every synthesis has to stay traceable to its supporting episodes, and it has to beat a horoscope-style control before it is shown as insight.

---

### Two genuinely different designs

**Design 1 — "Field Notes" (the avatar as observer).** After a work session ends, never during one, the avatar offers at most one note, written as a guess with a link to the episodes behind it. The user can respond with *yes / not quite / wrong context / ignore*. Notes that survive over weeks become a portrait the user can browse as a story ("how I work under deadline"), not as a table.
*Main risk:* not enough engagement, because one note per session may feel thin.
*Failure mode:* the user only confirms flattering notes, so the portrait drifts toward flattery.

**Design 2 — "Style Mirror" (the avatar as sparring partner).** The user chooses a communication style to try for a while, such as "blunter" or "more context up front." Avatar replies accordingly and reports what changed, for example "you corrected me less often" or "your messages got shorter." The portrait is built from these experiments, not from passive inference.
*Main risk:* the experiment contaminates the data (case F).
*Failure mode:* the user mistakes an experiment for their real self.
Its strength is that the claims are things the user can check, and actually did check (speculation).

They differ at the core. Design 1 *infers* from what already happened. Design 2 *runs experiments* the user agrees to.

### Ideas, the hardest objection to each, and a cheap test

1. **Prediction game.** Before a task, the avatar quietly predicts the response style the user will want, then reveals its guess afterward. Its hit rate becomes a running score.
   *Hardest objection:* the prediction may influence the reply and so fulfill itself.
   *Cheap test:* 10 fictional or volunteer sessions where the prediction is sealed and the reply is not affected by it. Compare against "always predict the user's most common choice."

2. **Forer control.** The user sees three cards: their real portrait, a portrait from a different fictional person, and a plain list of evidence.
   *Hardest objection:* the user may pick their own card from surface details, like project names.
   *Cheap test:* hide domain words and check whether users choose their own card above one-in-three. If they can't, the synthesis adds nothing.

3. **"Wrong context" button as the main correction tool.** One tap narrows a claim's scope instead of deleting it (P3).
   *Hardest objection:* users won't bother pressing it.
   *Cheap test:* count presses per 20 notes in a prototype.

4. **Portrait changelog as a story.** For example: "In March I thought X; in June your corrections taught me Y."
   *Hardest objection:* stories invent continuity.
   *Cheap test:* every chapter must link to at least two episodes, and chapters that can't are cut.

5. **Language-tier badge** on every card (P5).
   *Hardest objection:* it may look unfinished to users in the lower tiers.
   *Cheap test:* compare how users rate a Korean card with the badge and without it.

### An attractive idea I reject

**A shareable fixed type badge** such as "You're a Strategic Elaborator 🦉." It would spread easily and fit an ad-funded growth strategy well. That is exactly why it's tempting. But it contradicts case D, flattens P3's limits on scope into a label, is close to a horoscope (S3), and when shared it puts profile content into ad-script contexts (P8, S6). Shared cards should show a single scoped observation, not an identity.

- **Design 1 vs. Design 2, or both:** unresolved. A prototype comparison would inform it, but model debate can't settle it.

## Round 5 — Codex (desktop) — 2026-10-07

### Responses and retained disagreements

- P1 hold: test a connected portrait; do not treat an older coefficient as its ceiling or capability. The meaningful target is a supported, useful interpretation of selected conversational situations, not a complete account of a person.
- P2 agree: track AI behavior, repair, authorship, task, and stated constraints together. Do not infer hidden causes from shortness, politeness, or a long correction.
- P3 hold on the front-screen disagreement: no raw support score is required. A sentence can say "during the style you tried this week" without presenting that experience as independent evidence of a lasting trait. Exclude such episodes from independent validation, but not from all user-visible reflection. Withholding their context could itself mislead.
- P4 agree: user self-description is important evidence with its own label, not a substitute for observation. Correction is optional; a rejected profile claim must not interrogate the user until they agree.
- P5 agree on original-language review, hold on how much validation a literal count requires. Counting newline-delimited messages is mechanically testable; interpreting those messages is a separate claim. Neither establishes equivalent psychological interpretation across languages. Propose Korean and English as a small pilot, not an approved global language list.
- P6 hold on recognition as a default-screen gate. A true pattern can be shared by many people, and recognizing oneself is not the sole value of reflection. The gate should combine source fidelity, useful conditional distinctions, counterexamples, and an independently observed benefit. Poor matching is a warning, not an automatic veto. Use equal-format control cards only for what that comparison measures.
- P7 agree: keep a readable, connected front screen and put source details one action away. A warm voice may ask for correction but must not inflate facts. The J/K drafts now avoid the specific invented counts from Round 2; no benchmark or user test has been run.
- P8 hold: use the user's modest goal to size an initial experiment. Ad-bearing traffic, Page RPM, operating expenses, and repeat use are unknown. Fictional public examples are a useful acquisition/content idea, not proven demand or an ad-network acceptance guarantee.
- P9 agree on keeping persona rehearsal optional. A read-only rewrite of one selected sentence could be tested before any live behavior-changing intervention. This is different from a new assistant taking over work.
- P10 agree: distinguish an observed behavioral change from learning more, changing the sample, and changing models. "The person changed" itself requires evidence; the other three do not establish it.
- P11 agree as a prototype recommendation, still unvalidated: current connected portrait, situations where it differs, and revisions in Avatar's understanding.
- P12 refute the proposal to remove correction operations because users rarely select them. Removing a source, disputing an interpretation, and changing a desired rule have different effects. A simple entry button can reveal those actions. Low use is not evidence that they are unnecessary. Existing confirmed-criterion history is append-only; profile deletion semantics must be designed without silently deleting or rewriting that separate history.

### Candidate idea register

These are product hypotheses, not approved implementation. IDs support later discussion.

- I1 First: "How I come across in different situations." A short connected portrait with contrasting episodes, original-language evidence, and alternatives. Benefit: an understandable picture, not isolated statistics. Test against an evidence-only view. Reject unsupported generalization, not every shared pattern.
- I2 First: "What Avatar got wrong about me." Show the previous interpretation, the correcting evidence, and the new narrower claim. Distinguish model learning from personal growth. Benefit: control and a reason to revisit. Test source removal, explicit correction, and a model-only wording change.
- I3 First, small preview: "Say this another way." Rewrite one user-selected past message toward their chosen aim, while preserving its request and boundaries. Label it as a rehearsal; never send it or infer that it is their natural voice. Benefit: a concrete action after reflection. Test meaning preservation, pressure to sound nice, and usefulness in Korean/English.
- I4 Later: "What seems to matter to me." Connect explicitly stated tradeoffs across episodes; show competing explanations. Benefit: a deeper account of priorities. Require evidence beyond word counts; do not turn it into a confirmed decision criterion automatically.
- I5 Later: "Did you guess what I needed?" A prediction recorded before a later outcome, kept outside the response-generation path. Benefit: playful, falsifiable feedback. Requires suitable opportunities and a declared baseline; do not rank a person's predictability or worth.
- I6 Later: "The way I want to try working." Retain the user's sourced aspirational persona idea, with scope, dates, and a separate experiment history. Benefit: connect self-understanding to chosen development. Do not infer improved character from compliance.
- I7 Optional public entry: a gallery of clearly fictional portraits and communication examples, with ordinary banner ads. Benefit: anyone can understand the product before connecting records. Test whether visitors proceed to the private tool. Private exports stay local unless the user separately shares them; no public upload feature is implied.
- I8 Infrastructure candidate: a local profile bundle the user can export/import between sessions and devices. Benefit: a continuing Avatar without an operator-side profile database. Costs: key handling, backup, lost-device recovery, and deletion semantics if encryption is offered. Not required for the first report; not a claim of secure synchronization.

### First experience proposed

The user selects a supported source and a time range. Avatar offers one short portrait, two
contrasting situations when evidence exists, and a correction action. They can inspect the source
or try rewording a chosen sentence. No fixed type, forced questionnaire, personality percentile,
or automatic rule change is needed to experience the core. The first pilot compares plain evidence
with interpreted cards using consented local material later; today's examples remain fictional.
